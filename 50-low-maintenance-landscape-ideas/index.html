50 Low Maintenance Landscape Ideas

AN
Curated by AnnaDeco Studio
⏱ 6 min read · Updated 2026-05-10 · ✓ Editorial Verified
📌 Save Idea

Are you tired of spending every weekend weeding and watering?

Yard work shouldn’t feel like a second job!

I’m sharing 50 simple, stylish ideas to get you a beautiful yard that practically takes care of itself. Get ready to enjoy your weekends again!

1. Use Local Plants Only

These plants already love your local climate, so they need way less attention from you. Skip the constant watering and expensive fertilizers because they are built to thrive right where you live.

  • Plant what grows naturally here.
  • They resist local bugs better.
  • Saves money on soil amendments.

Pro Tip: Check your local agricultural extension office for a list of true native plants.

2. Hardy Perennials Save Water

Choose flowers and foliage that come back every year and survive with very little water. This cuts down on replanting costs and keeps your watering schedule simple and low.

  • Select drought-tolerant varieties.
  • No need to buy new plants yearly.
  • They establish strong, deep roots.

Pro Tip: Daylilies and Coneflowers are great, tough choices for sunny spots.

3. Ground Cover Replaces Grass

Replace traditional lawns with plants that spread naturally and hug the ground. This suppresses weeds and completely eliminates the need for a lawnmower, saving you hours.

  • Reduces or eliminates mowing.
  • Keeps weeds from sprouting up.
  • Looks lush without high effort.

Pro Tip: Creeping thyme smells amazing when you walk on it, a nice bonus!

4. Ornamental Grasses Add Texture

Use tall, architectural grasses for movement and visual interest in your yard. They look high-end but do not need frequent pruning or large amounts of watering to stay happy.

  • Needs minimal trimming.
  • Adds height without structure.
  • Provides winter interest.

Pro Tip: Plant them in groups of odd numbers (three or five) for a natural, flowing look.

5. Evergreen Shrubs Are Foundation

Plant shrubs that stay green all year long to give your yard structure through every season. They only need a quick trim once a year to keep their shape and color.

  • Keeps color even in winter.
  • Defines property lines easily.
  • Requires very little trimming.

Pro Tip: Boxwoods or Japanese Hollies are classic choices that always look neat.

6. Cacti and Succulent Gardens

If you live where it’s dry, succulents are your best friend. They create sculptural, modern areas that thrive on neglect and require almost no water at all.

  • Perfect for hot, sunny spots.
  • Needs watering maybe once a month.
  • Great for modern, clean looks.

Pro Tip: Use sharp gravel around them to keep moisture away from their base.

7. Climbing Plants on Trellises

Grow plants up instead of out to reduce ground maintenance and weeding. A simple trellis adds height and shade while keeping the growth contained and tidy.

  • Reduces clutter on the ground.
  • Adds privacy quickly.
  • Requires only occasional guiding.

Pro Tip: Wisteria is beautiful but can be aggressive; try clematis for easier care.

8. Use Hardy Herbs

Plant hardy herbs like lavender, rosemary, and thyme in sunny beds. They resist pests, smell wonderful, and tolerate heat and drought better than most flowers.

  • Smells great when the sun hits them.
  • Pests generally leave them alone.
  • Use them in cooking or crafts.

Pro Tip: Lavender only needs to be cut back slightly after it finishes blooming.

9. Low-Water Shrub Planting

Design your beds using shrubs known for surviving drought conditions. This greatly reduces your irrigation needs and keeps your water bill low.

  • Less reliance on the hose.
  • Shrubs provide year-round structure.
  • Plant them once and forget them.

Pro Tip: Look for shrubs labeled ‘xeriscape’—it means they love dry conditions.

10. Pollinator Friendly Zones

Select plants that naturally attract bees and butterflies, which helps your whole yard thrive. These plants usually grow well on their own with minimal human help.

  • Supports local wildlife.
  • Requires minimal intervention.
  • Adds beautiful, natural color.

Pro Tip: Planting Milkweed is crucial for supporting Monarch butterflies.

11. Wildflower Meadow Look

Replace part of your manicured lawn with native wildflowers. This area requires no mowing, very little watering, and offers seasonal color that looks effortless.

  • Eliminates mowing time.
  • Needs minimal water.
  • Creates a beautiful, natural boundary.

Pro Tip: Scatter wildflower seeds in the fall for blooms the following spring.

12. Shade Loving Ground Covers

Fill those tricky spots under trees or on the north side of your house with shade-tolerant plants. They grow without direct sunlight and need very little upkeep once established.

  • Perfect for deep shade spots.
  • Keeps weeds from growing.
  • Pachysandra is an easy choice.

Pro Tip: Amend the soil with compost once before planting to help them get started.

13. Strong Stem Perennials

Choose plants that have woody or very sturdy stems, so they won’t flop over when it rains. This means you don’t have to spend time staking or constantly cutting them back.

  • No need for staking or support.
  • Stays neat and upright.
  • Less cleanup after storms.

Pro Tip: Look for varieties of peony or baptisia that are known for strong stems.

14. Easy Bulb Planting

Plant bulbs that bloom reliably year after year without needing to be dug up or replanted. They offer cheerful spring color with zero annual effort from you.

  • They multiply on their own.
  • No annual replanting needed.
  • Great way to welcome spring.

Pro Tip: Daffodils are generally critter-proof and return reliably every season.

15. Slow Growing Trees

Pick trees that grow slowly and naturally maintain a manageable size and shape. This reduces the need for expensive, frequent pruning or massive leaf cleanup every fall.

  • Less frequent pruning needed.
  • Fewer leaves to rake up.
  • Stays proportional to your house.

Pro Tip: Japanese Maples are beautiful, slow-growing choices for smaller yards.

16. Minimize Grass Area

The biggest maintenance hog is usually the lawn, so reduce its size wherever possible. Replace large grass sections with hardscaping or mulch beds.

  • Lowers mowing frequency.
  • Saves money on fertilizer.
  • Reduces watering demands.

Pro Tip: Only keep the grass you actively use for playing or entertaining.

17. Tapestry Lawn Mix

Create a mixed lawn using low-growing plants like clover or creeping sedum instead of plain grass. This stays green and healthy with almost no mowing required.

  • Stays green during dry spells.
  • Requires minimal cutting.
  • Looks unique and intentional.

Pro Tip: Clover actually puts nutrients back into the soil, helping itself grow.

18. Creeping Ground Cover Lawn

Use specific spreading plants that act like grass but need much less water and cutting time. They fill in bare spots quickly and efficiently.

  • Less thirsty than turf grass.
  • Fewer weekly chores.
  • Stops erosion on slopes.

Pro Tip: Check if your chosen cover plant can handle light foot traffic before you commit.

19. Fake Grass, Real Savings

Installing quality artificial turf means zero mowing, zero watering, and always green color. It’s a big upfront cost but saves time and money on maintenance forever.

  • Roll out turf for instant lawn.
  • No more watering bills.
  • Perfect for small, tricky areas.

Pro Tip: Always put down a weed barrier layer first to keep it pristine.

20. Gravel Garden Spots

Use gravel instead of soil to reduce weeds, watering, and ongoing upkeep. Combine it with a few hardy, sculptural plants for a modern, clean look.

  • Weeds struggle to grow here.
  • Looks very high-end and clean.
  • No watering needed for the base.

Pro Tip: Choose a neutral gravel color like light gray or tan for a sophisticated feel.

21. Mulch Pathways

Install walkways made of wood mulch or bark chips to suppress weeds and reduce soil erosion. They are easy to install and only need fresh material every year or two.

  • Inexpensive and easy to install.
  • Looks natural and clean.
  • Great for shady areas.

Pro Tip: Use shredded hardwood mulch, as it stays put better than chips.

22. Native Meadow Zones

Allow designated areas of your yard to grow naturally using native plants. These zones require minimal human care, just occasional cutting down once a year.

  • Requires zero daily care.
  • Supports local wildlife.
  • Clearly define the edge to look intentional.

Pro Tip: Use a low fence or stone border to visually separate the wild zone from the patio.

23. Build a Rock Garden

Combine interesting stones and rocks with small, hardy plants like sedums. This provides great visual interest without requiring frequent watering or weeding time.

  • Very water-wise design.
  • Looks great all year long.
  • No need for rich soil.

Pro Tip: Bury the rocks slightly so they look like they’ve always been there.

24. Stepping Stones Between Plants

Install large stepping stones with low-growing ground cover planted between them. This creates a functional path and reduces the total area you need to mow.

  • Creates easy walking paths.
  • Reduces grass area instantly.
  • Looks charming and rustic.

Pro Tip: Ensure your stones are level and placed securely so they don’t wobble.

25. Flagstone Patio Space

Create an outdoor living space using flagstone or large pavers instead of planting. This area requires zero watering, weeding, or trimming forever.

  • Zero maintenance required.
  • Provides a clean living space.
  • Looks natural and expensive.

Pro Tip: Sweep sand or polymeric joint filler between the stones to lock them in place.

26. Water Plants Automatically

Drip systems feed water directly to the plant roots, which is super efficient and saves water. You set it once and let the system handle the watering schedule for you.

  • Hook up a simple timer.
  • Reduces water waste dramatically.
  • Your plants stay perfectly hydrated.

Pro Tip: Focus the drip line on new or high-value plantings first.

27. Group Plants by Thirst

Organize your yard by grouping plants based on how much water they need. This simplifies irrigation and ensures you don’t overwater the drought-loving varieties.

  • Easy to manage watering zones.
  • Improves plant health overall.
  • Saves time and water waste.

Pro Tip: Keep all your thirsty annuals close to the hose spigot or patio.

28. Rain Garden Design

Create a shallow depression filled with water-loving plants to capture rainwater runoff. This reduces drainage issues and means you don’t need to manually water that area.

  • Reduces water runoff problems.
  • Uses natural rain effectively.
  • Looks lush and natural.

Pro Tip: Make sure your rain garden is at least 10 feet away from your house foundation.

29. Permeable Paver Patios

Use special pavers that allow water to soak into the ground instead of pooling or running off. This prevents drainage headaches and looks beautiful.

  • Stops standing water issues.
  • Looks like a regular patio.
  • Helps replenish groundwater.

Pro Tip: These often use a layer of gravel underneath to help with drainage.

30. Thick Mulch Beds

Cover your soil with a thick layer of shredded wood mulch to lock in moisture and limit weed growth. This is the simplest way to cut down on weeding and watering time.

  • Keeps soil moist longer.
  • Blocks weeds from sprouting.
  • Looks neat and professional.

Pro Tip: Keep the mulch a few inches away from the base of trees and shrubs (no ‘mulch volcanos’).

31. Decomposed Granite Paths

Use compacted gravel paths that look natural, clean, and require almost zero maintenance. They create a smooth, firm surface for walking and entertaining.

  • Requires little upkeep.
  • Drains water quickly.
  • Gives a soft, natural look.

Pro Tip: Use a stabilizer product to keep the granite from washing away in heavy rain.

32. Easy Raised Garden Beds

Build beds off the ground to reduce weeds and simplify all plant care tasks. They look contained and professional while making gardening easier on your back.

  • Fewer weeds to worry about.
  • Controls soil quality perfectly.
  • Easier to reach and tend plants.

Pro Tip: Line the bottom with hardware cloth to keep burrowing pests out.

33. Large Container Gardens

Group oversized planters to control plant growth and simplify watering schedules. Containers can be moved, offering flexibility and keeping plants contained.

  • Plants can be moved easily.
  • Less weeding than in the ground.
  • Creates flexible focal points.

Pro Tip: The bigger the pot, the less often you have to water it.

34. Simple Pergola Structures

Install a simple wooden or metal structure to add shade and vertical interest. Structures add style without increasing your weeding or pruning chores.

  • Adds height and dimension.
  • Perfect for outdoor seating.
  • Requires only occasional sealing.

Pro Tip: Paint or stain the pergola a dark color for a modern, high-contrast look.

35. Permanent Edging Barrier

Install metal, stone, or durable plastic edging around all your planting beds. This prevents grass from creeping in and reduces the need for constant trimming.

  • Stops grass from invading beds.
  • Keeps mulch contained neatly.
  • Reduces string trimming work.

Pro Tip: Metal edging is thin, strong, and nearly invisible for a clean line.

36. Minimalist Seating Areas

Design outdoor seating surrounded by hard materials like pavers or crushed stone instead of flower beds. This creates a cozy spot without maintenance.

  • Focuses on comfort, not upkeep.
  • Zero watering or weeding needed.
  • Use simple, sturdy furniture.

Pro Tip: Use outdoor rugs to soften the look of the hard ground materials.

37. Boulders as Focal Points

Use large, decorative rocks or boulders as focal points in your yard. They add visual weight and interest without any need for watering or upkeep.

  • Instant, lasting focal point.
  • Requires zero care.
  • Looks intentional and natural.

Pro Tip: Cluster a few different sizes together rather than lining them up in a row.

38. Stepping-Stone Walkways

Create functional paths using spaced-out stepping stones set into the ground. This limits soil compaction and reduces plant damage from foot traffic.

  • Guides guests naturally.
  • Protects surrounding plants.
  • Easy to install yourself.

Pro Tip: Make sure the path is wide enough for two people to walk side-by-side comfortably.

39. Stone Mulch Instead of Wood

Replace organic wood mulch with river stones, pebbles, or crushed stone. This eliminates the need for yearly replacement and looks clean year after year.

  • Never needs replacing.
  • Great for drainage.
  • Perfect around fire pits.

Pro Tip: Put down a thick weed barrier underneath the stone layer before pouring.

40. Simple Water Features

Choose self-contained fountains or bubbling rocks that recycle their own water. They add soothing sound without requiring intensive cleaning or complex piping.

  • Adds calming sound.
  • Requires minimal cleaning.
  • Easy to set up and run.

Pro Tip: Place the water feature near your seating area so you can hear it easily.

41. Evergreen Structural Planting

Anchor your yard with plants that hold their form and color 365 days a year. This guarantees your yard always has good bones, even when other plants are dormant.

  • Provides winter structure.
  • Low need for pruning.
  • Holds the design together.

Pro Tip: Use varying shades of green and blue-green for subtle interest.

42. Naturalistic Plant Grouping

Arrange plants in loose, informal clusters instead of rigid rows. This reduces the pressure to prune perfectly and lets plants grow in a relaxed, natural way.

  • Less rigid upkeep required.
  • Looks effortlessly charming.
  • Mimics nature’s grouping.

Pro Tip: Let a few plants slightly spill over the edges of your beds for softness.

43. Wildlife Friendly Design

Encourage natural pest control by supporting birds, bats, and beneficial insects. A healthy ecosystem manages itself, reducing your need for chemicals and labor.

  • Reduces reliance on sprays.
  • Birds eat annoying bugs.
  • A truly self-sustaining system.

Pro Tip: A simple bird feeder can make a huge difference in attracting helpful birds.

44. Multi-Season Interest

Select plants that offer something interesting across multiple seasons, such as unique foliage or bark. You get maximum visual impact for minimal planting effort.

  • Keeps the yard looking alive.
  • Choose plants with red bark.
  • Enjoy color beyond just summer blooms.

Pro Tip: Hydrangeas have great flowers in summer and interesting seed heads in winter.

45. Potted Succulent Displays

Use potted succulents for flexible, low-care decorative accents on your patio or porch. They only need watering every few weeks and survive hot weather easily.

  • Easy to move around.
  • Needs minimal water.
  • Great for small spaces.

Pro Tip: Mix different textures and heights of succulents in one large pot for a big impact.

46. Texture Focus Over Flowers

Rely on the shape and texture of leaves (like hostas or ferns) instead of constant flowers. Foliage-focused beds stay attractive all season without needing deadheading.

  • No deadheading required.
  • Foliage lasts all season.
  • Creates a sophisticated, calm feel.

Pro Tip: Pair large, smooth leaves with fine, spiky ones for high contrast.

47. Curved Path Layouts

Design flowing, curved paths instead of straight lines. Curves look intentional and help hide natural wear or minor imperfections in the planting beds surrounding them.

  • Adds softness to the design.
  • Hides small errors easily.
  • Looks more natural than straight lines.

Pro Tip: Use a garden hose to plan out the path curve before you start digging.

48. Simple Color Palettes

Limit your flower colors to just one or two main shades (like white and blue). This simplifies planning, makes shopping easier, and looks consistently clean and neat.

  • Easier to plan and shop.
  • Looks clean and unified.
  • Prevents a chaotic feel.

Pro Tip: White flowers pop beautifully at night when you’re relaxing outside.

49. Wildlife Habitat Corners

Designate a low-care zone where you leave logs or natural debris to support local ecosystems. This corner needs zero maintenance from you but does a lot of good.

  • Requires zero care or effort.
  • Supports beneficial insects.
  • Looks like a natural woodland edge.

Pro Tip: Stack branches neatly in a corner so they don’t look messy or accidental.

50. Embrace Imperfection

Adopt a relaxed style that accepts natural growth patterns and minor flaws. Reducing the pressure for constant perfection instantly cuts down on your workload and stress.

  • Lower stress gardening.
  • Less pruning is always better.
  • Enjoy your yard as it is.

Pro Tip: If it’s healthy and alive, it’s beautiful. Stop comparing your yard to magazines!

See? Getting that high-end look doesn’t mean you have to spend all your free time pulling weeds or dragging a hose around.

By choosing smart plants and using hardscaping, you can easily create a beautiful, low maintenance yard that gives you back your weekends. Happy planting!
